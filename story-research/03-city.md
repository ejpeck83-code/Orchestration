# Setting: Lockport, New York

## The pick

**Lockport, New York.** Population 20,876 in 2020. It's a canal town and county seat on the Niagara Escarpment, about 20 miles from both Niagara Falls and Buffalo. Most Americans couldn't find it on a map.

In your world, Lockport is the center of a metro of a few million people, and it's where the rest of the country's district systems come from.

The surprise works because Lockport is small and forgettable here. It makes sense because Lockport already invented half of your world, and the other half turns on a real engineering decision made 20 miles away.

## Why it blossoms in your world

### 1. Lockport already invented the district model

- Birdsill Holly founded the Holly Manufacturing Company in Lockport in 1859. After fires in the city, he built a system that pumped water through mains to hydrants **with no reservoir or standpipe**, and over 2,000 cities adopted it ([NIHF](https://www.invent.org/inductees/birdsill-holly-jr)).
- In 1877 he built the first successful commercial district heating system in Lockport. By 1890 more than 50 were running, and district cooling with brine and ammonia followed ([Pierce 1995](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/87450)).
- Holly blasted a water-power tunnel under the city, now the Lockport Cave, between 1858 and 1900 to drive his factories ([Buffalo Rising](https://www.buffalorising.com/2021/09/2nd-in-a-3-part-series-life-underground/)).

In our history, the district model won downtown and lost the house. In your world it wins the house too, and the company that invented it is headquartered here.

### 2. The power fork happens next door

- In 1890-91 the International Niagara Commission, chaired by Lord Kelvin, ran a prize competition for sending Niagara's power to Buffalo (Jia & Crabtree 2015; Barnett 2020). The entries ranged "from a system using pneumatic pressure to one requiring ropes, springs and pulleys," plus direct-current schemes. The commission rejected them all ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/tesla/ll/ll_niagara.html)).
- Alternating current won the contract in 1893 and reached Buffalo in 1896. Steven Lubar argues the choice rested on engineers' cultural assumptions as much as on cost (Lubar 1989).
- City-scale pneumatic and hydraulic power already worked elsewhere. Paris ran compressed air through street mains, first to drive its clocks and then as a power supply ([Paris compressed air network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_compressed_air_network); Popular Science Monthly 1882). London ran water mains at 800 psi through about 180 miles of pipe from 1883 to 1977, powering lifts, cranes, theater stages and the backup machinery of Tower Bridge ([Grace's Guide](https://www.gracesguide.co.uk/London_Hydraulic_Power_Co)).

**Your fork:** the commission backs pneumatic and hydraulic distribution, or AC stalls in patent fights for 20 years. Air and water mains work across a city and lose too much over long distances, so Niagara's power stays near Niagara. Industry has to move to the escarpment.

### 3. Love's Model City gets built

- In 1893 William T. Love proposed a power canal from the upper Niagara River down the escarpment to Lewiston, feeding a planned industrial city. He projected as many as 2 million residents, broke ground in 1894, dug about a mile, and lost his investors by 1897 once long-distance transmission made the idea pointless ([Discover Niagara](https://www.discoverniagara.org/model-city-pre-wwii); [Lockport Union-Sun & Journal](https://www.lockportjournal.com/news/lifestyles/niagara-discoveries-the-dream-and-demise-of-model-city/article_e29081d6-e38b-5e3d-b7c6-67facbe2dc8a.html); Newman 2016).
- The unfinished ditch became Love Canal.
- The Model City area now holds a hazardous waste landfill that's operated since 1972 ([NYSDEC](https://dec.ny.gov/environmental-protection/waste-management/hazardous-waste/cwm-chemical-services)). On neighboring land from the same wartime ordnance works sits the Niagara Falls Storage Site, which holds Manhattan Project residues ([US Army Corps of Engineers](https://www.lrd.usace.army.mil/Missions/Projects/Article/3612360/niagara-falls-storage-site/)).

In your world, that ground holds the metropolis. In ours, it holds the waste. You never have to say so. A reader who looks it up will feel the floor tilt.

### 4. The escarpment is where the air gets made

- A hydraulic air compressor turns falling water into compressed air with almost no moving parts. The Ragged Chutes plant in Ontario did exactly that from 1910 for about 70 years ([Compressed Air Best Practices](https://www.airbestpractices.com/technology/air-compressors/hydraulic-air-compressor-old-idea-made-new); Millar 2014).
- Lockport sits on a drop of about 60 feet, with the Erie Canal on top. The Flight of Five locks lift boats up that face ([marker](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=189389)).

Put the compressor plants in the escarpment face, and Lockport becomes the city that makes the pressure everyone else runs on.

### 5. Every story in your list has a Lockport hook

- **The Intersection.** The escarpment and the canal split the city onto 2 levels, so it had to separate grades early. Its 1914 "Big Bridge" is 399 feet wide, with streets and a parking lot on its deck, and was called the widest bridge in the world ([HistoricBridges](https://historicbridges.org/bridges/browser/?bridgebrowser=other%2Fbigbridge%2F)).
- **The Number.** Transit Road runs through Lockport along the Holland Land Company's West Transit survey line, run around 1799 ([Lockport Union-Sun & Journal](https://www.lockportjournal.com/news/lifestyles/niagara-discoveries-holland-land-co-s-west-transit-line/article_57f64c3b-d3f3-5221-92f2-3e9efab10a89.html)). The region was a surveyed grid before it was a city.
- **The Dial and Cold Room.** Holly's 1877 steam system, plus Harrison Radiator: founded in Lockport in 1910, bought by GM in 1918, and at its peak employing over 10,000 people making radiators and car air conditioners ([Harrison Radiator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Radiator_Corporation)). In your world, Harrison builds district heat exchangers and chillers.
- **The Hydrant.** Holly's pumps and hydrants, and his system with no reservoir.
- **The Lift, The Lock, The Meter.** All run naturally on air and water mains.

### 6. A supporting argument: people stay north

In our history, home air conditioning helped pull Americans south (Arsenault 1984; Biddle 2008). Glaeser and Tobio (2008) argue housing supply and productivity mattered more. District climate is built for dense cities, so in your world the southward pull weakens somewhat and the Great Lakes keep more of their people. Treat this as supporting evidence. The power fork carries the main weight.

## What their Lockport might look like, 1962-82

These are prompts. The world is yours.

- **Upper Town and Lower Town**, joined by paternosters, ramps and stacked streets, because the city was built on 2 levels from the start.
- **Compressor halls in the escarpment face**, fed by the canal, with the hum of the plants as the city's background noise.
- **The Holly Works** as the recurring manufacturer whose nameplate is on everything: pumps, hydrants, lock mechanisms, paternoster drives.
- **Harrison** as the thermal company whose chillers sit in every basement.
- **A mirror you never explain.** Our Model City is a landfill. Theirs is the city.

## An easter egg for you

In 1887, the Ball brothers moved their glass-jar works from Buffalo to Muncie for free natural gas, a 7-acre site and $5,000 in cash ([Indiana Historical Bureau](https://www.in.gov/history/state-historical-markers/find-a-marker/ball-brothers-glass-manufacturing-company/)). Muncie's population roughly doubled during the gas boom, and in 1929 the Lynds made it *Middletown*, the typical American city ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Middletown-A-Study-in-Contemporary-American-Culture)).

In a world where cheap Niagara power stays near the Falls, the Balls might never leave Buffalo. Muncie never becomes Middletown. You could drop that into one story as a single line of background.

## Runners-up

**Niagara Falls, New York.** The obvious power pick. It had over 102,000 people in 1960 and 48,671 in 2020 ([Census](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/niagarafallscitynewyork)). It fits the power fork directly. It was also already a famous boomtown and a honeymoon punchline, so it surprises less. In your world it's the downtown next door.

**Holyoke, Massachusetts.** A planned water-power city whose power company sold water to each mill. Clemens Herschel ran the Holyoke Testing Flume, the first modern hydraulics lab, and invented the Venturi meter there to measure each mill's water, filing the patent in 1887 ([ASME landmark](https://www.asme.org/about-asme/engineering-history/landmarks/129-holyoke-water-power-system); [Herschel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clemens_Herschel)). Choose it if you'd rather build everything on hydraulic power by the pipe. The case for its growth is weaker.

**Muncie, Indiana.** The home-field pick: gas-boom city from 1886, the Ball brothers, *Middletown*. It fits a different fork, where natural gas is conserved and gas-fired district systems win. It fits your air-and-water world less well. Its big advantage is that you can walk it, and Ball State's Center for Middletown Studies is right there.

## Risks with Lockport

- **Buffalo is right there.** Readers from western New York will ask why Buffalo didn't become the giant. Your answer: the power is made on the escarpment, so the metro's center moves there. Buffalo still grows, as the lake port.
- **Love Canal is a real disaster with living victims.** Handle the Model City mirror with care.
- **Local historians will catch mistakes.** The Lockport Union-Sun & Journal's "Niagara Discoveries" column and the Niagara History Center are where to check details.
